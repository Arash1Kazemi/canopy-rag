AI Engineering Guides

Five pages that go deeper than the project docs. They are written for the person who wants to be a senior AI engineer, not only to finish the project. Each page is tied to your project, so every idea has a place to try it.

How these pages work
  • Try it here (green): a small exercise on your own project. Do it, it is where the learning happens.
  • Go check this yourself (blue): a place to read. I only list links I opened and confirmed exist. I do not summarize them, so you learn to read the source.
  • Senior habit (white): what separates a senior engineer from a junior one on this topic.
  • Careful (yellow): a mistake that is easy to make.

Ten rules a senior engineer keeps

  1. Measure before you change. If you cannot say how you will know it got better, do not change it yet.
  2. Look at the data. Read 50 real failures by hand before you tune anything. Most problems are visible in 20 minutes of reading.
  3. A number without an error bar is a guess. Small test sets move a lot by chance (see the Evals page).
  4. Change one thing at a time, and write down what you changed, the date, and the run id.
  5. Retrieval first, generation second. If the right text is not retrieved, no model can fix the answer.
  6. Keep the model behind an interface. Models change every few months. Your domain rules should not.
  7. Pin everything: model tag, prompt version, chunk policy, random seed, data version. A result you cannot reproduce is not a result.
  8. Fail loudly. A system that says "I do not know" is better than one that is confidently wrong. Test the "no answer" cases on purpose.
  9. Write down decisions and their reasons (you already do this: A1 to A20). Future you will not remember why.
  10. Learn by building small and reading the source. A weekend project that you finished teaches more than a course you half watched.
